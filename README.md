Conventional construction waste management heavily relies on Fixed Schedules or Reactive Schedules,
leading to severe logistical inefficiencies, site congestion, and excessive "empty mileage" that increase transportation costs
and CO₂ emissions. To solve these systemic failures, this study proposes a data-driven Dynamic Threshold Scheduling for
optimizing construction waste container logistics. A Monte Carlo simulation framework was developed and implemented
in Python, to evaluate the operational and environmental performance of different collection strategies across 1,000
randomized project scenarios. These scenarios vary in duration, waste generation rates, and container capacities. The
findings demonstrate that the Dynamic Threshold Strategy performs better than traditional methods by eliminating
overflow incidents while maintaining an optimal container utilization rate of approximately 75%. Furthermore, the
dynamic approach reduced total truck travel distance and the associated CO₂ emissions by nearly 50% compared to Fixed
Schedules. At the end Dynamic Threshold Schedule saves on average 185 working hours compared to Fixed Schedule.
While the theoretical model theoretically solves physical logistical trade-offs, its practical application necessitates the
integration of Internet of Things (IoT) sensors for real-time monitoring in the site. Ultimately, transitioning from static
methodologies to dynamic, data-driven approach transforms waste logistics from an operational liability into a strategic
project management advantage that aligns with modern sustainability protocols.
